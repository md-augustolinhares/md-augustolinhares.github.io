And after the incident?

Written on 21 Oct 2026, based on the hospital's statement of 8 Oct 2026.

Protecting health data also means knowing what to do after an incident.

In October, Hospital Albert Einstein reported unauthorized access to part of its patient database. According to its statement, this may include name, CPF (the Brazilian taxpayer ID), date of birth, prescriptions and test results. The hospital says it found no evidence of disclosure or misuse.

I read the statement as a physician who studies AI and data. What interested me most was the response.

The hospital says it blocked the mechanisms used in the access. Independent experts are part of the investigation. The case was reported to the ANPD (Brazil's data protection authority) and registered with the Federal Police. Affected patients receive individual notice by email. There is a portal where data subjects can ask questions.

No institution chooses to go through this. Any health service may have to respond to an incident.

So I leave a question for anyone who works in healthcare: if it happened tomorrow in my service, who does what, and in what order?

My view: the incident response plan should be as well known by the team as the cardiac arrest protocol.

With AI entering health services, data flows through more tools. That makes this preparation even more important.

Source: statement by Hospital Israelita Albert Einstein, 8 Oct 2026 (via Futuro da Saúde).
