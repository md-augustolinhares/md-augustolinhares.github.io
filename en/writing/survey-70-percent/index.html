“Almost 70% of physicians already use AI”?

Published on LinkedIn on 29 Sep 2026 (in Portuguese).

I saw a survey saying that almost 70% of physicians already use AI. But when I read it, I understood the base was 112 physicians. A classic overgeneralization bias.

The survey is by MV, a Brazilian healthcare software company. It heard 196 professionals across Brazil's five regions, between the second half of 2025 and early 2026. Of these, 112 were physicians, qualified or in training. And 74 of them said they use AI regularly or occasionally. That is where the 66.1% comes from, which became "almost 70% of physicians" on at least five websites.

Brazil has close to 635,000 physicians, according to the Demografia Médica 2025 projection. MV itself writes that the result is not statistically representative of the medical profession. The warning is there; it just disappears on the way to the headline.

That does not make the survey useless. It also matters that it comes from a company that sells technology to hospitals. A vendor survey gives me a hint about where to look, and that is how I read this one.

The part that interested me most was in the middle of the text. Among those who already use AI, 56.6% use it to support clinical decisions: interpreting tests, suggesting ICD codes, correlating symptoms. Visit transcription, which I imagined would be the entry point, comes well behind.

That worries me more than it excites me. No one measured whether those decisions got better. The survey measured adoption, and adoption is the metric the market loves, because it is easy to count and good for selling.

My view, and I know it is uncomfortable: we do not need more surveys counting how many physicians use AI. We need someone measuring how many treatment decisions changed, for better or worse, after AI entered the conversation.

Source: survey "O Uso de Inteligência Artificial por Médicos no Brasil" (MV Insights, 1st edition, Sep 2026), via Medicina S/A and Exame. Demografia Médica no Brasil 2025.
