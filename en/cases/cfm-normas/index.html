CFM Normas

A public archive of the Brazilian Federal Council of Medicine's norms in an open format (Markdown), with the validity status of each one, built for physicians, researchers, developers, lawyers and AI agents.

Limits, first. An organized reference source, not legal advice. An independent project, not affiliated with the CFM. It covers 331 resolutions, opinions and recommendations from 2020 to 2026; the 801 legal rulings are not in yet. The weekly automatic update is configured but has no recorded run yet. The norms are in Portuguese.

Purpose

The project exists, first of all, to give the public an open, reliable archive of the CFM's norms.

Historically, the medical profession faces a great scatter of information sources, which often makes it hard for health professionals to align and to share regulatory knowledge quickly.

I wanted to share that knowledge and bring the medical community, researchers, developers and lawyers closer around an accessible, reliable base. The repository makes the CFM's norms from 2020 to 2026 available in an open format, free to use.

The structure was designed for two readers: health professionals, and artificial intelligence agents that check ethical, regulatory and technical compliance in the practice of medicine.

Building the skills listed in the case card was the second purpose, and it comes with the first: the archive is the product, and the infrastructure shows how I think about a data product.

Problem

  • Scattered sources. Ethics norms, opinions on new procedures and rules on advertising and telemedicine sit across slow systems and loose PDFs.
  • Risk of citing a revoked norm. An AI agent that browses the web without a structured source may cite a norm that has already been revoked or changed. In medicine, following a norm that is no longer in force can lead to an ethics violation. That was the risk I wanted to reduce.
  • A portal that machines read poorly. The official portal shows PDFs inside a viewer, which makes them hard for AI agents and semantic-search pipelines to read without heavy scraping.

What I did

  • Archive: 331 CFM norms from 2020 to 2026 (196 resolutions, 134 opinions and 1 recommendation), each one in Markdown with a standard YAML header.
  • Validity: each norm carries its status (in force, revoked or suspended) and the identifiers of the norms it revokes. On 24 Oct 2026: 313 in force, 15 revoked and 3 suspended.
  • Themes: 5 macro-themes (such as medical ethics and digital health) and 17 tags (such as telemedicine, electronic health record and artificial intelligence).
  • AI and data track: a separate catalog with 53 norms that touch on artificial intelligence, data protection and telemedicine. Three flag AI, 33 flag data protection and 30 flag telemedicine; a norm can sit in more than one group.
  • Lookup by AI agents: the catalogs work like a dictionary. The agent asks for a tag, gets a list of identifiers and reads each norm straight from its URL, with no vector database and without downloading the repository. The full catalog is 433 KB; the AI track is 70 KB.
  • Automatic update: a GitHub Actions workflow, configured to run every Sunday at 01:00 (Brasília time), check for new acts and update the archive. There is no recorded run yet.

Decisions

  • The Federal Council, not the 27 regional ones. I chose the CFM, which issues the norms with national reach, and avoided maintaining 27 state portals that differ from one another.
  • The portal's own metadata. I found that the portal already delivers the metadata as JSON inside the page. I used that JSON instead of scraping HTML tables, which break easily.
  • No stored PDFs. Each PDF is read in memory and converted straight to Markdown. The repository holds no binary files.
  • A dashboard that opens with two clicks. The dashboard data is embedded in the file itself, so it opens straight in the browser, with no server.

Results

Measured on 24 Oct 2026:

  • 331 norms. That is every resolution (196), opinion (134) and recommendation (1) from 2020 to 2026 that the portal lists.
  • 4.37 MB for the norms in Markdown.
  • 3 min 40 s for the seven-year historical load (time measured by me).
  • R$ 0.00 per month: hosting and automation on GitHub's free plan.

Limits

  • Not legal advice. The archive organizes public data and helps anchor an AI in official sources. It does not replace legal counsel or a regional council's opinion on a specific case.
  • Validity as of the last update. The status holds for 24 Oct 2026. For a decision with consequences, check the CFM portal.
  • Partial scope. The archive has 331 of the 1,132 acts in the portal's count (29.2%). Resolutions, opinions and recommendations are complete; the 801 legal rulings are left for a later stage. Internal administrative ordinances of the CFM were left out.
  • The AI track is a theme filter. It points to where to look, but does not say what each norm requires. Only three norms flag artificial intelligence.
  • Automation not yet proven. The weekly workflow is configured, but the loads done so far did not go through it.
